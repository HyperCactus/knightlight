# Open-source ideas for Chess Study

Reviewed 6 November 2026. **Implementation update:** the app now includes continuation validation, visual evidence, spaced practice, Maia-3, Elo ratings, and a public practice pack. See the [current README](../README.md). The comparisons below describe the baseline at review time. This is a source-code review and implementation recommendation; the external applications were not installed or benchmarked. The recommendations below are proposed work, not features already integrated.

The strongest direction is to keep Rust and Stockfish as the foundation, add structured evidence for coaching, improve puzzle validation and scheduling, and evaluate a small human-move model for the opponent.

| Project and inspected revision | Useful approach | Fit for this app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [FOCALORS](https://github.com/Inuway/Focalors/tree/3a4cf14191c3dc7964babd80ef9c6b393eaf24f3) | Separate searchable evaluation from explainable position features; local persistence; adaptive difficulty | Closest Rust reference for coaching and review architecture |
| [Maia-3](https://github.com/CSSLab/maia3/tree/1e13597c42d4858b7cfd7cfdae01e297263364b2) | Skill-conditioned prediction of human moves, exposed through UCI | Most relevant candidate for a human-like opponent |
| [chess-coach](https://github.com/jcabiles/chess-coach/tree/de21fd8bf93641840bda9df7875759f7c1124e00) | Structured tactical facts, threat/plan narration, scheduled practice, alternative solutions | Useful examples of turning engine results into teaching |
| [Lichess puzzler](https://github.com/ornicar/lichess-puzzler/tree/8e24b6d5e2b9618e41581a327926e4ac67c610a2) | Check competing solutions and construct a continuation against engine defense | Useful quality gate before calling an extracted position a tactical puzzle |

**FOCALORS: make explanations concrete.** Its [evaluation breakdown](https://github.com/Inuway/Focalors/blob/3a4cf14191c3dc7964babd80ef9c6b393eaf24f3/src/eval.rs#L375) exposes material, mobility, pawn structure, king safety, and other components. Its [explanation builder](https://github.com/Inuway/Focalors/blob/3a4cf14191c3dc7964babd80ef9c6b393eaf24f3/src/analysis.rs#L704) combines material threats, the largest feature changes, and a legal engine line. This is a useful pattern for our currently limited capture/check/development explanations.

Implement a Rust `CoachEvidence` value containing the motif, involved squares, legal supporting line, and evidence source. Use it for the board highlights, text, and Piper narration so all three describe the same event. Prefer statements such as “the knight on e5 attacks the queen on d7 and rook on c6” over a generic evaluation warning. A handcrafted feature change is a positional observation; it does not establish the cause of Stockfish's evaluation change. Confirm tactical claims against legal continuations.

FOCALORS' [puzzle detectors](https://github.com/Inuway/Focalors/blob/3a4cf14191c3dc7964babd80ef9c6b393eaf24f3/src/puzzles.rs#L169) offer compact examples for forks, hanging pieces, and mating patterns. They are heuristics: attacking two valuable pieces alone does not prove a profitable fork. Its [adaptive controller](https://github.com/Inuway/Focalors/blob/3a4cf14191c3dc7964babd80ef9c6b393eaf24f3/src/strength.rs#L270) also demonstrates a minimum history window before adjusting difficulty. Our adaptation would benefit from a similar evidence threshold, with draws and coaching-assisted games handled explicitly.

**Maia-3: learn human move choices.** The [UCI implementation](https://github.com/CSSLab/maia3/blob/1e13597c42d4858b7cfd7cfdae01e297263364b2/maia3/uci.py) supports player/opponent skill, legal-move masking, temperature sampling, and optional reconstructed move history. The [project documentation](https://github.com/CSSLab/maia3/blob/1e13597c42d4858b7cfd7cfdae01e297263364b2/README.md) recommends its 5M model as the CPU starting point. The current app samples Stockfish candidates with style weights; Maia is a credible next step toward human move distributions.

Prototype Maia as a persistent UCI subprocess controlled by Rust, alongside the existing Stockfish coach. Preserve full game history. Set sampling explicitly: the preset launchers default to temperature zero. Keep a style preference stable for an entire game and vary it between games. Benchmark cold startup, warm move latency, memory, and several skill settings before selecting defaults. A proposed target is warm inference within the existing roughly one-second thinking interval on this machine; this has not been measured.

An integration trap: Maia's UCI centipawn field is derived from its predicted game outcomes. It is unsuitable as a replacement for Stockfish's searched evaluation in grading or puzzle validation. Model skill conditioning also needs local playing-strength calibration before showing an Elo estimate. Its [model card](https://huggingface.co/UofTCSSLab/Maia3-5M) documents human-game training; our personal error model can remain small rather than attempting neural training from 50 games.

**chess-coach: connect review to the next practice session.** Its [narrator](https://github.com/jcabiles/chess-coach/blob/de21fd8bf93641840bda9df7875759f7c1124e00/app/coaching.py#L25) returns separate threat, hanging-piece, plan, and summary fields. Its [motif output](https://github.com/jcabiles/chess-coach/blob/de21fd8bf93641840bda9df7875759f7c1124e00/app/motifs.py#L239) includes source and target squares, a useful contract for visual coaching. The exchange evaluator explicitly documents approximation limits, reinforcing the need for engine confirmation when making strong claims.

Its [trainer](https://github.com/jcabiles/chess-coach/blob/de21fd8bf93641840bda9df7875759f7c1124e00/app/trainer.py) schedules motif groups with 1/2/4/7/14-day intervals, rotates positions, and caps a session at ten puzzles. Our attempt counters currently change ranking but have no due dates. Add a short daily session with due weaknesses first, some fresh positions, and enough variation to test pattern recognition. Record first-try success, hints, reveal, and completion separately; viewing the answer must not count as unaided mastery.

Its [solution check](https://github.com/jcabiles/chess-coach/blob/de21fd8bf93641840bda9df7875759f7c1124e00/app/main.py#L2065) accepts alternatives within an evaluation window or when both positions remain winning. That is helpful for broad best-move practice, but a “still winning” rule can accept a missed tactical objective. Our validator should distinguish tactical puzzles from open-ended improvement exercises. The project also has an optional [cloud narrative module](https://github.com/jcabiles/chess-coach/blob/de21fd8bf93641840bda9df7875759f7c1124e00/app/narrative.py); its fluent narrative should not be taken as evidence of a local language model.

**Lichess: validate the puzzle and broaden the evidence.** The [generator](https://github.com/ornicar/lichess-puzzler/blob/8e24b6d5e2b9618e41581a327926e4ac67c610a2/generator/generator.py#L36) compares the best and second-best continuations, treats mate-in-one ambiguity specially, and builds lines against engine defense. Our puzzles currently accept one stored first move. Reanalyze candidates with MultiPV, store the continuation and objective, and replay the opponent's defense. Accept every move that satisfies the objective, including alternate checkmates. Positions with several equivalent choices can remain useful improvement exercises without presenting an arbitrary unique answer.

The [public Lichess database](https://database.lichess.org/#puzzles) supplies rated, tagged puzzles and evaluated positions under CC0. A small filtered puzzle pack could supply fresh examples of the same motifs found in your games. The [opening explorer](https://github.com/lichess-org/lila-openingexplorer) supplies played-game outcome counts; our installed named opening lines currently provide names and continuations only. If added, cache explorer results and show sample sizes and filters so popularity and win rate are not confused with engine quality.

**Recommended implementation order.** These priorities are engineering judgments based on the source review and current app code.

| Priority | Deliverable | Evidence required before calling it complete |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Fair puzzle checking and continuation play | Alternate mates accepted; equivalent moves handled; black-side scoring correct; a weak move that misses the objective rejected |
| 2 | Structured coaching with tactical highlights | Tests for forks, pins, defended captures, and legal supporting lines; text/audio/highlights share the same facts |
| 3 | Daily scheduled practice | Due dates survive restart; hints/reveals affect mastery correctly; sessions rotate examples and remain bounded |
| 4 | Maia-3 CPU prototype and opponent calibration | Measured latency/memory; legal play across skills; stable game style; strength changes gradually from enough completed games |
| 5 | Review summaries and public examples | Three useful turning points linked to practice; external puzzle provenance and database sample sizes visible |

Current implementation seams: [`describe`](../src/training.rs) for coaching facts; `Attempt`/`Learner` for scheduling; [`bot` and `attempt`](../src/main.rs) for policy and validation; [`app.js`](../static/app.js) for the puzzle continuation and board cues. Review cache keys must change when derived analysis changes, while puzzle attempt identities should remain stable across reanalysis.
